I walk around this building most every day. Even though I've lived here for five years, I continue to observe things I've never seen before. Perhaps that is an aspect that makes good architecture captivating.

## Overview

* Highly symmetrical
* Tripartite design
* Main entrance centered and expressed but not overly dominant

Note: In my mind, the plate glass door and window added in the 1950s that obscure the original recessed entry way are a major detraction from the original design.

### Horizontal Dominance

* Building stretches horizontally
* Continuous roof line
* Three stacked bands brick, parapet and roof
* Parapets of the three bay windows
* Three rows or bands of windows
* Windows sills and horizontal mullions

### Vertical Articulation

* The multitude of minor vertical elements
* Symmetry of the windows and their mullions

### Color Palette: Muted

* Bricks and terracotta, both a muted red, create a monochromatic effect
* Dark window reflections, dark gray slate roof, and the shadows of the terracotta elements add to the monochromatic effect
* Much more serenity than excitement

### Texture: Rich

* Everything has texture, even the leaded glass window sashes

## Claude Sonnet 4 says

**Compositional Balance:**

The architecture achieves a classic balance between horizontal stability and vertical interest. The horizontal dominance gives the building a sense of permanence and integration with the landscape, while the vertical elements prevent it from appearing squat or overly earthbound. This creates the dignified, institutional character typical of Colonial Revival civic architecture - grounded and substantial, yet architecturally dynamic.

The building essentially uses horizontal dominance with vertical articulation - a hallmark of successful institutional design.
